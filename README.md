Project Title: Hoop IQ – Smart Basketball Analytics System
 
Hoop IQ is a smart basketball training system that combines hoop-mounted sensors and computer vision to automatically detect and analyze shot attempts in real time. The system uses an ESP32-based hoop sensor subsystem (IR break beam + piezo vibration detection), a Raspberry Pi 5–based computer vision subsystem implemented in Rust, and a web-based dashboard hosted at hoopiq.shop for data visualization and analytics.

The project addresses the lack of affordable basketball analytics tools for everyday players and small programs. Professional shot-tracking systems are costly and inaccessible to most users. Hoop IQ provides meaningful performance data—make/miss detection, shot classification, shot frequency, and trajectory insights all using low-cost embedded hardware and open-source software.

The team selected this project because it integrates embedded systems, firmware development, signal processing, computer vision, and full-stack software design into a cohesive real-world application. The interdisciplinary nature of the project provides a technically challenging opportunity to design, build, and demonstrate a complete end-to-end engineered system.

Beyond the technical scope, this project carries a personal meaning for the team. We all grew up playing sports, watching the game, and being around people who genuinely dreamed of making it — friends and people around us who put everything into getting better with whatever they had. The tools that could have helped them train smarter, track progress, or understand their own game simply were not accessible. Professional analytics systems exist, but they are built for programs with budgets most players and communities never see. Hoop IQ was our way of building something we wished had existed — a tool that gives serious players real feedback without requiring serious money.
 
The system was fully integrated and demonstrated across three subsystem demos and is prepared for a final integrated demonstration in Demo 4 and at the Engineering Senior Project Expo. All core subsystems are operational and we learned a lot through the entire process of it all.
